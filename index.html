Why it works

Grounded in how memory actually works.

Active recall beats re-reading

Pulling an answer out of your own head is one of the most reliably effective ways to learn. Passive highlighting feels productive but fades fast.

Spaced repetition fights forgetting

Revisiting a topic just as you're about to forget it strengthens the memory each time. The plan spaces your reds so they resurface at the right moment.

Rating turns dread into a plan

Naming what you don't know is weirdly calming. Once every topic has a colour, "I'm so behind" becomes a short, specific list you can actually work through.

These are well-established principles from learning science — no magic, no gimmicks. The app just makes them easy to follow when it's late and you're tired.

Questions

Good to know.

It's a study tool that turns the NSW HSC syllabus into a checklist you can rate. You colour each dot point by how confident you feel, flag your priorities, and get a personalised plan that focuses your time on your weak spots.
Every syllabus dot point gets a colour: red means you don't know it yet, amber means it's shaky, green means you're confident. Tapping cycles through them. Seeing your whole course in colour makes it obvious where to spend your next study session — and watching reds turn green as you learn is genuinely motivating.
Fifteen subjects across the main faculties: Biology, Chemistry, Physics and Health & Movement Science; Maths Advanced and Standard 2; English Advanced and Standard; and Business Studies, Legal Studies, CAFS, Modern History, Ancient History, Studies of Religion and Visual Arts — every dot point mapped from the current syllabus.
Your ratings are yours. Your confidence colours and priorities are saved for your use so you can pick up where you left off — they're not sold or shared, and the whole thing is designed to work quietly in the background of your revision.
The dot points are drawn from the current NSW HSC syllabus for each subject, so what you're rating matches what you're actually examined on. It's a study companion, not an official NESA product — always check your school's guidance for the final word on your course.
