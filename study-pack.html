§1How to use this pack

Five parts: where you stand (colour it in), an English self-assessment, a Visual Arts game-plan, the study recipes (the how-to), then the week.

  • Start with §6 (the recipes). Every block in your week is really one of nine recipes. The day plan gives the topic; the recipe gives the moves.
  • Times are suggestions, not law. Slot the blocks into whatever your day looks like. Order and balance matter more than the clock.
  • Work in ~1–1.5 hr blocks with a proper break between (the first one always runs long — that's normal). Reds get your freshest hours.
  • Re-colour as you go. A topic isn't "done" when you've read it — it's done when you can explain it with the notes closed. Click it up to green when that's true.

§4English Advanced — self-assessment you can traffic-light

A whole module is too big to colour in one go, so it's broken into skills. Click the circle on each row to rate yourself. Any orange/red is a target this week.

The exam: Paper 1 = Common Module (Texts & Human Experiences) — unseen short-answers + an essay (50 marks, 1.5 hrs). Paper 2 = Module A + B + C, one response each (25 marks each, 2 hrs).

Common Module — Texts & Human Experiences Paper 1

I can name my text's form, composer & the human experiences / emotions it explores
I have 6–8 quotes with techniques, and know what each reveals about human experience
I can discuss the "anomalies, paradoxes & inconsistencies" in human behaviour the text shows
I can write a thesis that answers an unseen question, not retells the text
I can analyse an unseen text fast (Section I): technique → human experience
I can plan & write a full essay in ~40 min

Module A — Textual Conversations Paper 2

I know both texts, their forms & contexts
I can explain how the later text reimagines / resonates with the earlier
I have 3–4 comparison points with paired evidence
I write integrated comparison, not text-by-text

Module B — Critical Study Paper 2

I know the text as a whole, not just fragments
I can argue its textual integrity / why it's valued
I understand different critical readings of it
I have my own informed judgement I can defend

Module C — The Craft of Writing Paper 2

I have 2–3 polished pieces (imaginative / discursive / persuasive) I can adapt
I've studied my mentor texts' stylistic features & can borrow them
I can write to an unseen stimulus under time
I can write a reflection statement justifying my craft choices
Do this first (Monday, after pilates): spend 30–45 min colouring every row. Any orange/red becomes a target this week.

Every written question makes you use three toolkits. Colour how confident you are with each:

Structural frame — signs, symbols, composition, how it's made
Postmodern frame — appropriation, irony, questioning conventions

5+ case studies I can write about from two frames each
Section I — fast short answers on unseen plates
Section II — a timed essay using my case studies
Fastest win: if you can confidently apply all four frames to two artworks, you've covered most of what Section I & II reward. Start there.

§6Study recipes — how to actually get it in your brain

You don't need more hours — you need the right moves. The golden rule: you don't learn by reading, you learn by retrieving. Forcing information out of your memory is what makes it stick. Every block in your week is one of the recipes below.

The Study Loop — for any content topic

1 · LearnOpen notes to the exact syllabus dot point. Read once to understand. ~10 min.
2 · CondenseClose the book. Rebuild it in your own words — mind map, flowchart, or "explain to a 12-year-old". ~10 min.
3 · TestEverything closed, answer a question on it from memory. ~10 min.
4 · FixCheck against notes in another colour. Gaps become tomorrow's flashcards. ~5 min.

The nine recipes (your week points to these by letter)

ALearn a content topic — HMS & Bio reds, Module 7

  1. Run the Study Loop on the exact syllabus dot point.
  2. For HMS "explain / discuss / assess" points, finish with 3–4 exam-ready dot points + one real example.
  3. Say it out loud once with everything closed. If you can teach it, you know it.

BMaster a problem type — pedigrees, Punnett, allele frequencies

  1. Copy out one worked example step by step.
  2. Cover it and redo the same one from scratch.
  3. Do 5–8 fresh questions.
  4. Re-do only the ones you got wrong until automatic.

CPrep an English module essay

  1. 3 past questions; underline the key word ("to what extent", "how").
  2. Write a thesis answering one in a single sentence.
  3. List 3 techniques + quotes that prove it.
  4. Write one body paragraph: point → quote → analysis → link to question.

DTimed exam practice

  1. Timer on. Essay = 5 min plan · 35 min write.
  2. 10 min self-mark against the criteria: answered the question? used the concept? analysed technique? sustained argument?
  3. Write your one biggest fix at the top. That's the point.

ECraft of Writing (Module C)

  1. Take one device from a mentor text.
  2. Write a short piece to a past stimulus using it deliberately.
  3. Add a 3–4 sentence reflection. Keep 2–3 flexible pieces for exam day.

FBuild a Visual Arts case study

  1. One page per artist: artwork · materials · intention · world/context · audience · best 2 frames.
  2. Cover it and say the profile from memory.
  3. Aim for 5+ artists, each usable from two frames.

GVisual Arts Section I

  1. Look at the plate 30 sec — subject, materials, composition.
  2. Read the question + its mark value (3-marker ≠ 6-marker).
  3. Answer using frame/framework language, referencing what you can see. 3–4 per sitting.

HVisual Arts Section II

  1. Deconstruct the question — which agency or frame is it about?
  2. Pick 2 case studies that fit.
  3. Plan 3 paragraphs (point + case-study evidence), then write it timed.

IActive recall & flashcards

  1. Make question → answer cards for facts you keep blanking on.
  2. Test, shuffle, re-test the ones you miss.
  3. 10–15 min, high yield. Great for tired evenings or travel.

§8Five things that'll make the week work

  • If you only remember one thing: close the notes and make yourself recall it. The struggle to remember is the learning.
  • Do problems for problem subjects. Pedigrees, Punnett, allele frequencies — learned by doing (Recipe B).
  • Time your essays. Running out of time is the #1 trial trap — that's why English gets short timed blocks (Recipe D).
  • Protect the greens. One past question keeps a strong topic warm. Don't let comfy topics eat your red hours.
  • Fuel it. Batch-cook so meals don't eat study time, keep the gym, and stop when a day's blocks are done.
